# Learn to Build Software — Practice and review

Use [the overview](OVERVIEW.md) to define a useful project and the
[learning outcomes](CURRICULUM.md#core-modules) to identify what you want to
demonstrate. Explanation, discussion, and existing project work can each provide
useful evidence. Choose the depth and pace that serve your question.

## Evidence of understanding

An explanation in your own words, a prediction, or a comparison can show how you
understand an idea and its limits. Receiving an explanation or repeating AI's
answer establishes less than reasoning about a different case. Record what is
actually shown and what remains open.

## Evidence of application

Choose a small relevant task in your app, or bring existing work. Explore the
idea with the teaching AI, then apply it independently with your implementation
agent in **your own project repository**. Give the agent your intended behavior,
expectations, and constraints. Make choices, question proposals, and check the
results. AI may write the code; your requests, decisions, and follow-ups can
show how you applied the outcome.

A useful handoff carries the selected outcome or question, intended behavior,
necessary constraints, and evidence worth bringing back. Use it when helpful;
you do not need a formal template or a fully solved prompt. Small explanations
and examples can stay in the teaching conversation. App implementation belongs
in your independent project, using its tools and checks. No course fork or
website installation is needed.

## Bring work back for review

Ask the teacher to review the relevant work against a selected outcome. You can
return after independent work or bring something you did before a lesson. Supply
enough of the following to support the question:

- **Context:** the outcome or question, task, and important expectations or
  constraints.
- **Your direction:** the relevant implementation chat link, pasted excerpt, or
  exported transcript showing your requests, decisions, and follow-ups.
- **Project evidence:** the repository and commit or changed files, relevant
  tests and their results, and observations of what the person or client can do.

The teacher must check what it can actually access. A chat link is a pointer,
not automatic access to another conversation. If it cannot open a chat or
private repository, supply the relevant excerpt, files, or diff instead. Keep
credentials and private details out of shared material. A full transcript is
unnecessary when a short portion supports the claim. Missing code or results can
limit a review without making it useless.

For example: you decide that a spaces-only book title should leave the list
unchanged, ask your agent to check that case, and return the exchange plus the
test and observed result. The teacher can assess your expectation and choice of
case. If you supply only the agent's “all tests passed” message, it can
acknowledge that reported result while leaving your reasoning unassessed.

## Feedback against the outcome

The teacher can use **review-learning-evidence** in a course reference checkout,
or follow [the teaching guide](AI-TEACHER.md) with supplied course material. It
should identify specific demonstrated judgment, explain its effect on the work
where visible, and name the most useful gap or improvement.

Keep separate what the software evidence establishes, what you demonstrated, and
what the AI supplied. Agent-written code and green tests alone do not prove
understanding. Partial evidence supports a limited conclusion, not a mastery
claim; missing evidence is not proof of inability. A focused question or small
retry can clarify a genuine gap without repeating checks that already answer it.
Distinguish reported results from inspected evidence.

Keep progress and learning notes in your established private record home. In a
local teaching reference checkout, ignored `.learning/` is an option after
checking that its files are untracked; it is not automatically backed up.
Project repositories have their own ignore rules. Preserve partial progress, AI
assistance, uncertainty, and useful return points without publishing private
transcripts. Use recoverable experiments and disposable data.
